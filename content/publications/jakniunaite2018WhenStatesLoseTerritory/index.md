abstract: 'In this article I analyse how Georgia, as a political entity, coped with
  the de facto loss of two of its territories: Abkhazia and South Ossetia. The process
  by which Georgia lost these territories started in early 1990 and reached its final
  phase in 2008 after the Georgian-Russian war. This article explores how Georgia
  adjusted to these losses without ever acknowledging its loss of the two territories,
  demonstrating a perfect example on how the normative territorial structure of an
  international system works. The analysis focuses on the crucial role of time in
  the process of the de facto territorial changes and examines how Georgia, in adapting
  to territorial losses and through its own actions, actually strengthened its separation
  from Abkhazia and South Ossetia.'
