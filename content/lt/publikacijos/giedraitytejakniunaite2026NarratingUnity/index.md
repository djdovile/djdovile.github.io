abstract: 'Russia''s invasion of Ukraine in February 2022 intensified debates over
  the future of the international order. Previous research has examined media coverage
  and Russian narrative justifications, but little is known about how Western political
  leaders initially constructed their own strategic narratives in response. Applying
  the strategic narratives framework, this article investigates how leaders in seven
  Western states and the EU framed the invasion and its implications for the liberal
  international order during the first month of the war. At the system level, leaders
  relied on general invocations of the rules-based international order. At the identity
  level, their calls to defend "their order" reaffirmed a Western community of democratic
  values and personalised blame. At the issue level, policy responses were validated
  through appeals to unity and solidarity. By repeatedly asserting unity, Western
  political leaders aimed to reassure audiences of the West''s coherence. The invasion
  consolidated the West as a political actor, but at the cost of limiting its legitimacy.
  By tying Ukraine''s defence to Western identity, leaders struggled to extend the
  persuasiveness of their narratives towards a broader audience. The article demonstrates
  how narratives functioned as mechanisms for maintaining order: safeguarding the
  West''s identity and sustaining established international structures.
