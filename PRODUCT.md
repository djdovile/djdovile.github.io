# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The site serves colleagues, students, prospective course participants, media contacts, project partners, and readers who need to understand Dovile Jakniunaite's academic work, public writing, teaching, projects, and contact routes. Visitors usually arrive with a specific information need: verify expertise, find publications, learn what she teaches, read occasional blog material, reach an official profile, or enter a course/newsletter pathway.

## Product Purpose

This is a public academic profile and archive for Dovile Jakniunaite. It should make her expertise legible, credible, and easy to navigate across publications, projects, teaching, public interventions, blog posts, future online-course gateways, and a Buttondown newsletter subscription path. Success means a visitor quickly understands who she is, what she works on, what material is available, and where to go next.

## Brand Personality

credible, clear, expert

The site should feel like a serious academic home base: calm enough for reading, structured enough for research discovery, and confident without turning into a promotional portfolio.

## Anti-references

Avoid startup/portfolio flashiness, generic Hugo Blox template feel, decorative academic cliches, overdesigned personal-brand aesthetics, gratuitous cards, loud hero theatrics, and visual treatments that make scholarship look like marketing copy. Avoid burying useful archive navigation under decorative design.

## Design Principles

- Credibility before decoration: make authorship, institutional context, publications, and project evidence easy to trust.
- Lithuanian-first clarity: prioritize the Lithuanian public site while keeping English parity as a later, deliberate layer.
- Archive as interface: treat publications, blog posts, projects, courses, and public talks as navigable records, not generic content cards.
- Expert restraint: use visual identity to support confidence and orientation, not to compete with the writing.
- Clear onward paths: surface official profile, course gateways, newsletter subscription, and contact routes without turning them into sales funnels.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Aim for at least WCAG 2.2 AA contrast and keyboard accessibility. Design choices should support long-form reading, bilingual navigation, Lithuanian diacritics, English content, reduced-motion preferences, and clear link affordances for older archive material.
